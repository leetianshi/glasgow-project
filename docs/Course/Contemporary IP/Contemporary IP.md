### 2.1 Seminar 1
<span style="color:#B00020;">**[Chunck 010101]**</span><br>
**Key words**: copyright weaponization; noncopyright interests; market exclusivity; economic incentives; legal strategy<br>
**Essay-ready point**: Copyright weaponization occurs when copyright is invoked not to protect market exclusivity or economic incentives, but to advance noncopyright interests such as suppression, retaliation, reputation management, or privacy protection.<br>
**Original Sentence**: All these stories have one thing in common: they all involve copyright owners using copyright to advance noncopyright interests. This Article refers to these actions as ‘weaponizing copyright.’<br>

<span style="color:#B00020;">**[Chunck 010102]**</span><br>
**Key words**: copyright weaponization; utilitarian copyright; incentives; dissemination; market exclusivity; public benefit<br>
**Essay-ready point**: The article contrasts weaponized copyright with copyright’s conventional justification: copyright protects authors’ market exclusivity so that economic incentives will encourage socially valuable creation.<br>
**Original Sentence**: Copyright grants authors exclusive rights in their works in order to encourage creation and dissemination of socially valuable works.<br>
**Page**: 195

<span style="color:#B00020;">**[Chunck 010103]**</span><br>
**Key words**: copyright weaponization; economic interest; licensing market; copyright purpose; misuse; non-economic claims<br>
**Essay-ready point**: A central critique is that weaponized copyright does not serve copyright’s traditional function because the claimant is often not trying to preserve a licensing market or recover economic loss.<br>
**Original Sentence**: In none of these stories are individuals asserting copyright to protect their market exclusivity or economic interests — the intended purposes of copyright.<br>
